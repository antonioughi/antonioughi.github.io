Pre-Doctoral Working Papers

  • Ughi, A. and Mina, A., "The Returns to Advanced Digital Capital: Evidence from an Industrial Policy". R&R at Research Policy.
    Abstract

    The latest wave of digitalization — encompassing advanced robotics, smart sensors, and artificial intelligence — is widely regarded as a key driver of future productivity growth. Yet evidence on the returns to investments in these technologies remains scarce, a gap that is particularly salient as governments increasingly subsidize firms' digital adoption. Using confidential firm-level administrative data, we exploit variation generated by the Nuova Sabatini, a major Italian industrial policy program, to estimate the returns to advanced digital "Industry 4.0" investments over 2012–2024. The program offered two investment tracks, one for digital and one for conventional capital, allowing us to estimate both the effects of each type of investment and the differential returns to Industry 4.0. We employ staggered and repeated-treatment difference-in-differences designs to estimate the effects of investments, and a stacked specification to identify the differential returns to Industry 4.0. We find that both types of investments increase capital accumulation, employment, and debt, but reduce productivity in the short run, consistent with adjustment costs. On average, Industry 4.0 yields no additional gains relative to conventional capital, except in intangible capital accumulation. These average differential effects, however, mask substantial heterogeneity: initially less productive firms experience gains, whereas initially more productive firms decline. We interpret these patterns in a theoretical framework where firms farther from the technological frontier obtain short-run gains from digital investment, while firms closer to the frontier undertake complementary investments with potential J-curve returns. The findings suggest that assessments of technology-targeted industrial policy should account for firms' initial productivity and the timing of investment returns.
